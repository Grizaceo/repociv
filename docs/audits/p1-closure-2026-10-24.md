## The reopens (why the history looks contradictory)

1. The first closure (`c6d54b2`, 2026-10-14) declared R03 and R12 closed, but
   no production module imported `process_control.py` or `task_recovery.py`.
   The 2026-10-18 audit reopened both.
2. `b6791bb` (2026-10-18) wired them for real and made the validator
   fail-closed. That is the closure that counts.
3. The 2026-10-25 adversarial review reopened **R01**: a failure between the
   single-use consume and `claim_intent` left an unclaimed intent holding the
   only recoverable copy, and a duplicate approval response deleted it before
   restart recovery could replay it. The fix keeps that intent alive and adds
   a regression that walks resolve → claim failure → duplicate response →
   restart.

The adversarial review [`p1-review-r01-r05.md`](p1-review-r01-r05.md) also
blocked the first R01 close (pop→claim window and a duplicate-dispatch path).
The protocol order today is: record the durable intent → consume the
single-use approval → claim the intent → audit/enqueue → release. The intent
is **recorded** before consumption and **claimed** after it; the claim is what
marks "already consumed" across a restart. `enqueue_dict` dedupes against
queued, persisted and already-dispatched commands under `_queue_lock`.

## What this closure does not claim

- **Model routing is computed, not enforced.** `server/model_router.py` and
  `server/step_executor.py` select a tier and fallback chain per step and
  record it in `step_meta["model_routing"]`; `server/step_retry.py` escalates
  the chain on retries. The selected model is not yet injected into
  `agent_runner.run_agent`, and the router is called without `mission_text`.
  This is a pre-existing gap, not an R01–R16 item; `SCOPE.md` and
  `COMPARISON.md` now state the limitation instead of claiming enforcement.
- **R01 guarantees dispatch-level delivery, not exactly-once external side
  effects.** The documented limit stands in
  [`../DURABLE_APPROVAL_DELIVERY.md`](../DURABLE_APPROVAL_DELIVERY.md).
